General practice in England · June–August 2026

Urgent appointments seen the same day

The 2026/27 GP contract requires practices to deal with clinically urgent requests on the same day, measured against a 90% target. In June to August 2026, 77.5% of urgent appointments in England took place on the day they were booked. Practices in deprived areas, with more urgent demand and fewer GPs, were least likely to reach 90%.

Where practices stand

Most practices are below the 90% target

Across England, 77.5% of urgent appointments took place on the day they were booked. The median practice reached 82%. 29% of practices reached 90%, and 55% reached 80%.

Urgent appointments with a GP were more often on the same day (81%) than those with other practice staff such as nurse practitioners and pharmacists (71%).

For every practice to reach 90%, about 848,000 urgent appointments a month would need to take place on the day of booking rather than later.

Need

Deprived practices have more urgent demand and fewer GPs

Practices in the most deprived fifth of England record 16% more urgent appointments per patient than the least deprived, with 16% fewer GPs per patient. Their patients have twice the smoking, three-quarters more COPD and nearly two-thirds more serious mental illness.

The median practice in the most deprived fifth saw 79% of urgent appointments the same day, against 85% in the least deprived. 76% of the most deprived practices did not reach 90%.

GP staffing

Practices with more GPs are more likely to reach 90%

33% of practices with the most GPs per patient reached 90%, against 22% of those with the fewest.

After adjusting for deprivation, age, sex, smoking, other practice staff and how many appointments are recorded as urgent, each extra full-time GP per 10,000 patients was associated with 1.0 percentage points more urgent appointments seen the same day (95% CI 0.7–1.4), and 11% higher odds of reaching 90%.

Each GP in the most deprived practices already provides 229 same-day appointments a month, against 188 in the least deprived.

Funding

Core funding is 19% below 2008/09 in real terms

In 2008/09 practices received £99.13 per person in core contract and QOF funding. Had that kept pace with inflation it would be £152.94 in 2023/24. Practices received £123.70. Across England that is £1.69bn a year, enough for about 15,500 salaried GPs.

Based on the associations above, those GPs would raise the share of urgent appointments seen the same day from 77.6% to about 80.1%, and the share of practices reaching 90% from 29% to about 34%.

Restoring funding would help, but on its own would not bring most practices to 90%. The target therefore needs to take account of capacity and need.

Recommendations

Restore core funding, weighted to need

Return core contract funding to its 2008/09 real-terms value, about £1.69bn a year, weighted towards deprivation and early-onset illness.

Core + QOF per person: £123.70 now, £152.94 if it had kept pace with inflation.

Assess the 90% target against capacity and need

Publish each practice's result alongside its GPs per patient and urgent demand, and do not penalise practices with the fewest GPs and highest need.

Practices reaching 90%: 24% most deprived, 35% least deprived.

Expand GP training in deprived areas

Fund supervision time, premises and educators so that more practices in deprived areas can train GPs.

Practices training GPs: 48% vs 66%, most vs least deprived.

Make recording consistent

Issue clear national guidance on which appointments count as clinically urgent, so that results reflect access rather than differences in how slots are mapped.

Appointments recorded as urgent: under 6% in one in ten practices, over 41% in another one in ten.

Methods and limitations

The urgent same-day measure

This follows NHS England's method for the 2026/27 contract: appointments in the national category General Consultation Acute that took place on the day they were booked, as a share of all General Consultation Acute appointments. All appointment statuses are counted. Excluding missed appointments makes little difference (79% rather than 77.5% nationally).

  • Practices decide which requests are clinically urgent and map their appointment slots to national categories. Practices differ in how they do this, so part of the variation between practices reflects recording rather than access.
  • The measure counts appointments. Urgent requests resolved without an appointment, or sent to other services, are not included.
  • Practice figures combine June, July and August 2026. Practices with fewer than 30 urgent appointments a month are left out of distributions and models because their percentages are unstable. They are still shown individually.
  • Practices with fewer than 1,000 patients, implausible appointment volumes or over 40% unmapped appointments are left out of charts and totals.
Strength of the evidence

The associations with deprivation and GP staffing are consistent but modest (rank correlations of −0.12 and 0.12). The adjusted model explains about 3% of the variation between practices in the urgent same-day measure. Most of the variation reflects how practices organise triage and record appointments, which national data does not capture.

The adjusted estimates come from a linear model (percentage seen the same day) and a logistic model (reaching 90%) across 5,222 practices. They adjust for deprivation, age 65+, age 0–14, % male, smoking, nurse, other clinical and admin staffing, and the share of appointments recorded as urgent. Confidence intervals allow for practices in the same ICB being alike.

A rank correlation runs from −1 to +1. Zero means no association. It measures the strength of an association, not statistical significance, and is not a p-value.

  • These are associations across practices at one point in time. They are consistent with GP numbers affecting same-day care, but they do not prove it.
  • Staff employed by PCNs through the ARRS scheme are not attributed to practices in the workforce data.
Funding shortfall calculation
  • 2008/09 England core contract + QOF funding: Global Sum + MPIG, PMS baseline, APMS/PCTMS core and QOF, £5,136m (NHS Digital, Investment in General Practice 2008/09–2012/13). QOF is included because QOF points were later moved into the global sum. The 2023/24 figure counts the same items (NHS Payments to General Practice).
  • Uprated by ONS CPI (×1.543, financial-year averages) and England's population (×1.119), then compared with what was actually paid.
  • Seniority pay was also later moved into the global sum but cannot be separated in 2008/09. It is left out, which makes the estimated shortfall smaller than it is.
  • Funding for PCN-employed staff (ARRS), premises, IT and dispensing is outside the core contract and not counted.
  • Each practice's share is the national shortfall divided by weighted patients. Salaried GP cost is the midpoint of the BMA model contract range plus employer NI and the employer pension contribution practices pay (£109,658).
  • The estimated effect on the urgent same-day measure applies the adjusted association to each practice's additional GPs per 10,000 patients (about 2.4 on average). It assumes the association is causal and that the GPs could be recruited.
Definitions and data sources
  • Deprivation fifths: practices ranked by IMD 2025 practice score and split into five equal groups.
  • GPs per 10,000 patients: qualified GPs (excluding GPs in training) in full-time equivalents, November 2025.
  • Other practice staff: all non-GP clinicians and staff recorded in appointment data, including nurse practitioners, practice nurses, pharmacists and paramedics. The national data does not separate these roles.
